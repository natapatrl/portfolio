Hi, I'm Natapatr. Nat for short.

I'm a game designer. Level design and UX/UI are my favorites.

How did you get into game design?

Frustration, mostly.

I played a lot of games, and what bugged me most were questionable dev decisions.

One mobile game I used to play changed its UI and font three times in a month. I had to relearn it every time, and misclicks cost me matches.

Others reworked characters into something more basic, and the charm was gone.

Before game design, I was set on becoming an artist. When that didn't work out, I applied to KMUTT with nothing left to lose. I did surprisingly well, so I stayed.

So what would you do?

Keep the skill ceiling, lower the floor. Usually that means less for the player to remember and process at once.

And don't make players relearn what they already know. Changes get built around the old layout, like button placements and keybinds.

How do you approach design?

I break things down to the fundamentals first: why they work, and where they fail.

Then I look at it from a completely different angle. Same song, different instrument.

How do you decide what goes in?

It has to serve the game's design pillars. If it can't, it usually doesn't make the cut, unless it's a big QOL win.

When does challenge become frustration?

When it's overly repetitive, but mostly when effort goes unrewarded. Every action should get a reaction.

That's why I'd rather add levels that teach each step than hold the player's hand. Figuring it out yourself is the reward.

What's your style?

Understated. I like being different, just not loudly.

What I aim for is quiet elegance. A design has to pass my own standards before anyone else's, even if it already works.

What tools do you use?

Unity and Unreal (incl. UEFN), and Figma for UI prototypes.

Confluence for design docs, Jira and Trello for sprints.
